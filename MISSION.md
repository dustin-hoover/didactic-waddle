# Mission — why this exists, and what it owes back

Saatgut ("seed corn") is a self-custody, evolutionary trading system. Building wealth
is the near-term goal. But the honest truth about this game must shape what we do with
any winnings:

## Trading is (at best) zero-sum, so gains carry an obligation

Speculative trading does not create value — it transfers it. After fees, spread, and
MEV, the pool of participants nets *negative*. Any profit this system earns came out of
someone else's pocket. That's not a reason to stop; it's a reason to **give back on
purpose**, and to give in a way that actually creates value the market never did.

## The tithe: a fixed give-back, executed as "teach a man to fish"

- **A tithe on realized gains** (target ~10%, operator-set) is set aside as a first
  claim on winnings — before compounding, not after — so giving is structural, not an
  afterthought.
- It is spent on **capability, not charity-as-consumption**: the mantra is *teach a man
  to fish*. Concretely, the chosen cause is **helping people grow their own food** —
  seeds, tools, and knowledge for household/community food resilience. Saatgut is
  literally "seed corn"; the name is the promise. A bag of seeds, planted, that grows
  more seed — for others, not just for us.
- It should be **transparent and verifiable** (on-chain where sensible: a dedicated
  give-back address, public tallies) and **directed to teaching/enabling** — gardens,
  seed libraries, growing know-how — over one-time handouts.

## When

This is deliberately deferred: **build and prove the machine first**, then wire the
tithe. It is recorded here so it survives context resets and is not forgotten. When the
system is running with a real, honest track record, the give-back is the next feature —
not optional, not an afterthought: the reason the whole thing is worth building.

_"Sow · cultivate · multiply" — and then hand out seed._
