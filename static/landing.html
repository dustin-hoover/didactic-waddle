The mechanism

Four rules, run without emotion

Every candle, the bot runs the same loop. No hunches, no revenge trades, no “this time is different.” That consistency is the entire point.

01

Read the regime

Is price above its trend line (a slow EMA, with a buffer to ignore noise)? If yes, hold the asset. If it breaks below, move to cash. The bot is only exposed while the trend is working — it never argues with the market.

02

Cap the downside

Every position gets a hard stop placed a multiple of ATR (recent volatility) below entry. Losers are cut small and automatically. A separate circuit breaker liquidates to cash if the account ever draws down too far — and re-enters only after price recovers.

03

Take money off the table

Each time the account makes a new high, a fixed slice of the new gain is sold into strength and swept into a stable reserve (think USDC). Selling winners on the way up is what turns paper gains into kept gains.

04

Compound the safety

The reserve only ever grows — a market crash can’t claw it back — and it earns yield while it sits (a stablecoin lending position, or staking AMPL / SPOT). You compound safety, not just exposure.

05

Spin the flywheel

Once the banked reserve grows to a multiple of your original seed (default: it triples it), a share of it (default: two-thirds) is injected back into the trading pool — enlarging the base that earns the next round of profit, which refills the reserve faster, which fires the next injection. The machine ratchets itself up, and keeps a third banked, until you stop it.

Why it holds up

Borrowed from the traders who actually lasted

None of this is invented. Each rule maps to a principle proven over decades by people who kept their money. We borrow their principles — not a claim to their returns.

Dennis turned novices into millionaires on a mechanical trend system; Seykota’s trend funds compounded for decades. Managed-futures desks have run this playbook profitably for 40+ years.

Jesse Livermore · Paul Tudor Jones

Cut losses, let winners run

“Where do I get out? — I ask that before I get in.”

Tudor Jones hunts five-to-one payoffs and obsesses over the exit. Asymmetry — many small losses, a few big wins — is how trend traders survive being wrong most of the time.

Our rule 02. ATR stops cut losers small; the trend lets winners run.

“Rule No. 1: never lose money. Rule No. 2: never forget Rule No. 1.”

Buffett is a long-term holder, not a trader — so we borrow his risk principle, not his method. The idea that defense compounds better than offense is the spine of this whole system.

Our rules 02–04. Drawdown breaker + a reserve that never gives money back.
Ed Thorp · the Kelly criterion

Thorp beat blackjack and then the market for decades with tiny drawdowns by sizing to the edge and banking gains. Position sizing and taking profit are not afterthoughts — they’re the strategy.

The fine print, out loud

What this is not

Every honest strategy has a cost. Hiding it is how people get hurt, so here it is plainly.

  • →Not a top-caller. It will not sell the exact top or buy the exact bottom. It captures the middle of trends and gives back the edges.
  • →Not a bull-market winner. In a straight-up market, buy-and-hold beats it on raw return — the chart above shows exactly that. Its edge is a far smaller drawdown, not a bigger number.
  • →Not risk-free. Even with every guard on, a real crash still hands the trading book a painful drawdown. Only the banked reserve is truly protected.
  • →Not a guarantee. A backtest is not a forecast. Fees, slippage and regime changes are real, and past results never promise future ones.
