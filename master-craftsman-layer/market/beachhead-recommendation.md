# Beachhead selection — findings (partial, real sources)

**Status:** two of six candidates researched against real primary sources
(state licensing statutes/rules + BLS OEWS wage data). The other four are
flagged pending the *same* pull. Confidence is labeled per cell. Dimension (b),
regulatory permissiveness, is a **gate**, not just a score — and the early
findings show it is the dimension that actually decides this.

## The headline finding

The three things you'd want to align — best labor arbitrage, operator's home
turf, and a permissive regulatory posture — **do not align across these
candidates**, and the divergence runs straight through the core model:

- **Best labor arbitrage:** Kansas City (HVAC median ~$30.14/hr, 90th pct
  ~$42–45/hr). Highest journeyman wage = most headroom over a $35/hr augmented
  tech… but note KC's *median* is already near your augmented-tech target,
  which compresses the arbitrage at the low end.
- **Operator's backyard:** NW Arkansas / Fort Smith (Arkansas).
- **Most permissive for the model:** Arkansas — and, critically, the obvious
  operator-adjacent alternative, **Oklahoma (Tulsa), appears to structurally
  break the model** (see below).

## Labor arbitrage (dimension a) — real BLS OEWS

HVAC mechanics & installers (SOC 49-9021). "Headroom" = distance between the
local journeyman wage the model displaces and the $35/hr augmented tech.

| Metro | Median hourly | 90th pct (master/foreman) | Arbitrage read |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kansas City, MO-KS | ~$30.14 | ~$42.68 | Highest headroom at the top end; median already near target |
| Tulsa, OK | ~$28.25 | ~$37.69 | Moderate |
| NW Arkansas (state-anchored) | ~$23.90 (AR median) | ~$45 (Fayetteville est.) | **Lowest journeyman wage — smallest arbitrage,** but lowest cost base too |
| Greenville / Boise / Chattanooga | pending | pending | pending |

Note the tension: NWA has the *most permissive* law but the *thinnest* wage
arbitrage, because Arkansas journeyman wages are already low. The model still
works there on absolute cost, but the "$120/hr → $35/hr" headline compresses to
roughly "$24/hr journeyman → $35/hr augmented + amortized master," which is a
*quality/coverage* play more than a raw labor-cost play. Be honest about that in
the pitch.

## What this means for decision D1

1. **Oklahoma/Tulsa should probably drop** unless counsel finds a license
   structure (e.g., operating under a mechanical *contractor* license with
   journeymen, not apprentices) that survives the 3:1 direct-supervision rule.
   Confirm before committing.
2. **Arkansas/NWA is the strongest regulatory fit and your home turf**, at the
   cost of thinner wage arbitrage. The whole thesis's legal viability is most
   defensible here — which, given "one wrong splice ends the company," is worth
   a lot.
3. **Kansas City is the arbitrage winner** but its regulatory posture is
   unresearched and it straddles two states (KS + MO) — double the licensing
   diligence.

## Recommended top-2 to bring to founder (D1)

**A. NW Arkansas** — best regulatory footing + operator advantage; accept
thinner arbitrage. **B. Kansas City** — best arbitrage; contingent on a
clean regulatory read for *both* KS and MO.

**Do not treat this as decided.** Both finalists still require the dimension-(b)
